Executive Problem Statement & Core Thesis

In credit card risk assessment, accuracy is an inherently misleading metric. In our dataset of 29,965 Taiwanese credit card clients, default occurs at a base rate of $22.13\%$. A trivial rule predicting "nobody defaults" achieves $77.87\%$ accuracy with $0\%$ recall, resulting in a 100% loss of unpaid customer balances.

Real-world credit decisions are heavily asymmetric: failing to detect an actual defaulter ($c_{FN}$) results in total balance write-off and legal collection costs, whereas a false alarm ($c_{FP}$) results only in temporary account friction. Under Bayes decision theory, the optimal decision threshold is:

$$t^* = \frac{c_{FP}}{c_{FP} + c_{FN}} = \frac{1}{1 + r}, \quad \text{where } r = \frac{c_{FN}}{c_{FP}}$$

When a missed defaulter is $5\times$ more costly than a false alarm ($r = 5$), the Bayes cost-minimizing threshold is $t^* = \frac{1}{1 + 5} \approx 0.167$. Evaluating models under the standard symmetric threshold of $0.50$ leads to severe economic underperformance.

Section 01

1. The Question We Are Answering & What One Row Represents

What One Row of Data Represents

Each row represents exactly one unique individual credit card client of a Taiwanese commercial bank between April 2005 and September 2005. The record captures their static credit limit and demographics, followed by longitudinal monthly payment statuses, bill amounts, and payment amounts across six consecutive months. The target variable indicates whether that specific client defaulted on their credit card payment in the subsequent month (October 2005).

Primary Research Question

"Using a client's repayment status, bill amounts, payment amounts and demographics from April to September 2005, can we predict whether they default next month, and which decision threshold minimizes expected cost when a missed defaulter costs more than a false alarm? Are the predicted probabilities calibrated, and how do regularized logistic regression, an SVM, random forest and boosting compare, including how ridge and lasso treat the six monthly bill-amount columns, which I expect to be strongly correlated?"

Honest Scope & Claim Boundaries

  • Non-Causal Association: Predictive markers of delinquency do not prove causation (e.g. higher age does not cause financial solvency).
  • Temporal & Regional Snapshot: Data comes from a period of heavy credit-card expansion and default stress in Taiwan (2005–2006). Base rates and coefficients may not generalize to other macroeconomic regimes.
  • Delinquency Benchmark, Not Lending Origination: The model assesses delinquency risk among existing account holders; it is not designed to approve or deny initial credit applications.
  • No Fairness by Unawareness: Excluding protected demographic features (`SEX`, `MARRIAGE`) does not eliminate disparate impact due to structural correlations. We measure outcomes through post-hoc subgroup error auditing.

Data Quality Issues & Resolution Protocol

  • Exact Duplicate Rows (35 Instances): While the arbitrary sequential ID column gave the illusion of 30,000 distinct rows, checking across all 23 financial and demographic variables revealed exactly 35 identical records. These were dropped before splitting to eliminate test contamination.
  • Column Naming Anomaly (`PAY_0` $\to$ `PAY_1`): The raw file labeled September repayment as `PAY_0`, skipping directly to `PAY_2` for August. We renamed `PAY_0` to `PAY_1` to ensure consistent 1-to-1 temporal alignment with `BILL_AMT1` and `PAY_AMT1`.
  • Undocumented Categorical Codes:
    • `EDUCATION`: Documented values are 1..4. Undocumented values {0, 5, 6} (345 rows) were recoded to 4 ('Others').
    • `MARRIAGE`: Documented values are 1..3. Undocumented value {0} (54 rows) was recoded to 3 ('Others').
  • Severe Collinearity in Monthly Bills: Bill amounts from adjacent months exhibit extreme pairwise correlations ($r = 0.955$), yielding Variance Inflation Factors (VIF) up to $17.08$.
Section 03

3. Preprocessing Choices & Statistical Justifications

Every preprocessing transformation was designed to satisfy statistical principles and strictly eliminate data leakage:

1. Standard Scaling of Numeric Features

Why: Continuous variables have wildly disparate scales (e.g. `LIMIT_BAL` and `BILL_AMT` exceed 500,000 NT dollars, while `AGE` is 20–70 and `PAY` is -2 to 8). Regularized linear models (Ridge, Lasso) and distance-based SVMs are scale-dependent; without scaling, features with large nominal values dominate the regularization penalty.

2. One-Hot Encoding with Dummy Drop

Why: `EDUCATION` is categorical without equal-interval properties. We apply OneHotEncoder(drop='first'). Dropping the first dummy avoids the "dummy variable trap" (perfect multicollinearity) for unregularized linear models.

3. Isolation of Protected Attributes

Why: `SEX` and `MARRIAGE` were removed from feature matrix $X$ prior to model fitting to prevent explicit demographic disparate treatment. They were preserved in a parallel metadata dataframe for post-hoc fairness auditing.

4. Leakage-Free Pipeline Encapsulation

Why: Calling fit_transform on the full dataset before cross-validation contaminates the training set with test set mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma$. We wrap all transformations inside Scikit-Learn Pipeline objects, ensuring scaling parameters are fit strictly on training folds.

4. Baseline & Candidate Models Compared

We intentionally select distinct model families to study inductive bias, collinearity handling, and probability calibration:

Model Name Role Inductive Bias & Core Mechanism Justification for Inclusion
Majority Class Accuracy Baseline Zero-rule model: predicts $\hat{y} = 0$ for every client. Demonstrates the "accuracy fallacy": scores 77.87% accuracy with 0% recall and catastrophic cost.
Flag Nobody / Flag Everybody Cost Policy Baselines Heuristic policy: either intervene on 0 clients or intervene on all 100% of clients. Establishes analytical break-even cost ratio ($r = 3.52$). A model must beat both trivial policies.
Plain Logistic Regression Linear Reference Unregularized maximum likelihood estimation of log-odds. Provides an interpretable, unconstrained linear probabilistic benchmark.
Ridge Logistic ($L_2$) Regularized Linear Penalizes sum of squared weights ($\lambda \sum w_j^2$). Shrinks weights of correlated monthly bills proportionally together, stabilizing coefficient variance.
Lasso Logistic ($L_1$) Sparse Linear Penalizes sum of absolute weights ($\lambda \sum |w_j|$). Drives small coefficients to exact zero, selecting one representative bill month and testing sparsity.
Elastic-Net Logistic Blended Linear Convex combination of $L_1$ and $L_2$ penalties. Maintains the grouping stability of Ridge while retaining the feature elimination power of Lasso.
RBF-Kernel SVM Non-Linear Margin Radial Basis Function kernel mapping to infinite-dimensional Hilbert space + Platt scaling. Tests non-linear margin separation. Platt scaling fits a univariate sigmoid to produce calibrated probabilities.
Random Forest Bagging Ensemble Aggregates 150 decorrelated bootstrap decision trees with feature sub-sampling. Reduces variance in non-linear decision spaces; robust against outliers and extreme bill values.
HistGradientBoosting Sequential Boosting Histogram-binned gradient descent minimizing log-loss pseudo-residuals sequentially. Reduces bias by focusing consecutive iterations on difficult minority-class defaulters. Top performer.
Section 05

5. Evaluation Design: The Split & The Metrics

Validation & Split Design

  • Stratified 80/20 Partition: Fixed once with random_state=42 into 23,972 training clients and 5,993 test clients. Default rate is preserved at exactly 22.13% in both partitions.
  • Sealed Test Partition: The test set was never accessed for feature selection, hyperparameter tuning, or threshold selection.
  • Stratified 5-Fold CV: Hyperparameters were tuned inside the training set across 5 folds. The tuning objective was cross-validated Log-Loss because the cost decision rule relies fundamentally on probability quality.

Why These Metrics Fit the Question

  • PR-AUC (Average Precision): Accuracy and ROC-AUC are inflated by the large volume of True Negatives. PR-AUC isolates performance directly on the minority default class.
  • Log-Loss & Brier Score: Strictly proper scoring rules that penalize overconfident mispredictions.
  • Equal-Frequency ECE: Expected Calibration Error using quantile bins ensures low-risk probability regions are evaluated fairly.
  • Expected Financial Cost: Cost per client $= \frac{FN \cdot r + FP}{N}$. Direct translation into banking loss.
  • 1,000 Bootstrap Resamples: Generates empirical 95% Confidence Intervals for all headline metrics without refitting.

Section 07

9. Probability Calibration & Reliability Curves

Reliability Curves (Equal-Frequency Quantiles)

Equal-frequency quantile binning is essential because ~78% of credit clients cluster at low default probabilities.

Why Calibration is Mandatory for Cost Thresholding

The mathematical formula $t^* = \frac{1}{1 + r}$ holds if and only if predicted probabilities represent true empirical frequencies ($P(y=1 \mid \hat{p} = q) \approx q$).

  • HistGradientBoosting: Achieves outstanding calibration ($\text{ECE} = 0.0114$, $\text{Brier} = 0.1348$), aligning tightly with the ideal diagonal.
  • RBF-SVM with Platt Scaling: Fitting a sigmoid over margin scores transforms geometric distances into calibrated probabilities ($\text{ECE} = 0.0295$).
  • Class Weighting Warning: Training with class_weight='balanced' artificially shifts predicted probabilities toward 0.5, destroying calibration and making the Bayes formula invalid.

10. Error Analysis: False Positives vs. False Negatives

Deep inspection of representative client misclassifications at threshold $t^* \approx 0.167$:

False Positive Profile (False Alarm)

Client Archetype: Revolving Balance User Settling Debt

Characteristics: Clients with high credit limit utilization ($>85\%$) and past revolving credit (`PAY_1 = 1` or `2`), who made a large lump-sum payment (`PAY_AMT1` large) in September.

Why the Model Was Tricked: The historical payment delay (`PAY_1`) and high utilization generated an elevated predicted risk ($p \approx 0.22 > t^*$), but the recent balance liquidation allowed the client to stay current in October.

False Negative Profile (Missed Defaulter)

Client Archetype: Sudden Unheralded Solvency Shock

Characteristics: Clients with spotless 6-month repayment records (`PAY_1` through `PAY_6` all $\le 0$) and moderate credit utilization, who defaulted without warning in October.

Why the Model Was Tricked: Without macroeconomic transaction logs or employment status, the model assigned a low risk ($p \approx 0.08 < t^*$). The default was triggered by external solvency shocks invisible in credit bureau billing records.

Section 09

11. Limitations of What We Found

Data & Structural Limitations

  • No Calendar Date Column: The UCI dataset provides only month offsets (April to September 2005) without calendar timestamps, preventing true temporal walk-forward backtesting.
  • Taiwan Credit Card Crisis Context: Data coincides with the 2005–2006 Taiwanese cash-card debt crisis. The baseline default rate ($22.13\%$) reflects elevated macro stress and may not represent tranquil credit cycles.
  • Credit Limit as Endogenous Proxy: `LIMIT_BAL` reflects past underwriting decisions by Taiwanese lenders, partially capturing bank risk appetite rather than borrower nature.

Decision Model Limitations

  • Fixed Cost Ratio Simplification: We evaluate constant cost ratios $r \in [1, 10]$. In commercial credit management, loss is exposure-dependent ($\text{Cost}_{FN} \approx \text{Outstanding Balance} \times \text{LGD}$).
  • No Interventions: The dataset captures passive outcomes; it cannot evaluate whether proactive bank interventions (e.g. credit freezes, payment restructuring) alter default probabilities.
