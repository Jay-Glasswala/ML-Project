The Core Thesis & Motivation

In financial risk assessment, accuracy is a dangerously misleading metric. In our credit dataset of 30,000 Taiwanese clients, defaults occur at a rate of ~22.1%. A trivial rule predicting "nobody defaults" scores 77.9% accuracy with 0% recall, resulting in total loss of unpaid principal balances.

Real-world default decisions are asymmetric: missing an actual defaulter ($c_{FN}$) is far more costly than flagging a good customer for credit review ($c_{FP}$). Under Bayes decision theory, the optimal decision threshold is:

$$t^* = \frac{c_{FP}}{c_{FP} + c_{FN}} = \frac{1}{1 + r}, \quad \text{where } r = \frac{c_{FN}}{c_{FP}}$$

When a missed defaulter is $5\times$ more costly than a false alarm ($r = 5$), the optimal decision threshold is $t^* \approx 0.167$. Evaluating models under the naive default threshold of $0.5$ leads to massive financial loss.

Section 01

1. Research Question & Claim Boundaries

Primary Research Question

"Using a client's repayment status, bill amounts, payment amounts and demographics from April to September 2005, can we predict whether they default next month, and which decision threshold minimizes expected cost when a missed defaulter costs more than a false alarm? Are the predicted probabilities calibrated, and how do regularized logistic regression, an SVM, random forest and boosting compare, including how ridge and lasso treat the six monthly bill-amount columns, which I expect to be strongly correlated?"

Honest Claim Boundaries

  • No Causal Claims: Features are predictive markers of delinquency, not causal mechanisms.
  • Geographic & Temporal Bounds: Data represents a single 2005 snapshot during Taiwan's credit-card expansion and stress cycle. Unconditional base rates will shift across economic regimes.
  • Not a Lending Tool: This model is an operational risk benchmark for account monitoring, not a credit underwriting engine.
  • No Fairness by Unawareness: Excluding protected demographic features (`SEX`, `MARRIAGE`) from inputs does not guarantee outcome parity. We conduct post-hoc audits to measure error rates directly.

Section 03

3. Leakage-Free Preprocessing & Pipeline Architecture

1. Fixed Stratified Split

80% Train (23,972 rows) / 20% Test (5,993 rows) split once with random_state=42. The test set was sealed until final confirmation.

2. Feature Matrix Isolation

SEX and MARRIAGE were stripped from feature matrix $X$ and held in audit metadata. EDUCATION and AGE remain active inputs.

3. Scikit-Learn Pipelines

All continuous scalers (StandardScaler) and categorical encoders (OneHotEncoder) are wrapped inside pipelines and fit strictly on training folds inside 5-Fold CV.

4. Automated Verification

Passing test suite (pytest) verifies disjoint indices, absence of protected variables in $X$, and leak-free re-fitting.

Collinearity Dynamics: Ridge vs Lasso

Due to pairwise correlations among bills ($r$ up to $0.955$):

  • Ridge ($L_2$): Smoothly shrinks all six bill coefficients together, keeping them balanced and preserving historical continuity.
  • Lasso ($L_1$): Induces sparsity by eliminating redundant bill months. In bootstrap resamples, Lasso consistently retains `BILL_AMT1` (the most recent balance) while driving intermediate months toward zero.
  • Elastic-Net: Yields stable non-zero coefficients without erratic dropouts.

Section 05

7. Probability Calibration & Reliability Analysis

Reliability Curves (Equal-Frequency Bins)

Equal-frequency quantile binning is utilized because ~78% of clients have low default risk. Standard equal-width bins would leave higher probabilities empty.

Why Calibration Matters for Cost Thresholds

The decision threshold $t^* = \frac{1}{1 + r}$ relies strictly on the mathematical assumption that if a model outputs $p(x) = 0.20$, exactly 20% of such clients actually default.

  • Tree Ensembles (Random Forest / Boosting): Tend to push probabilities away from 0 and 1 due to leaf averaging, but HistGradientBoosting achieves excellent log-loss and well-aligned quantiles.
  • SVM Platt Scaling: Fitting a sigmoid over decision margins produces smooth probabilities that conform to empirical base rates.
  • Class Weighting Warning: Training with class weights artificially inflates probabilities, destroying calibration and rendering formula-based threshold shifting invalid.
Section 06

8. Error Inspection, Limitations & Claims Integrity

Representative Error Profiles at $t^*$

  • False Positives (False Alarms): Clients with a single past delayed payment (`PAY_1 = 1` or `2`) who recently experienced a sharp balance drop (`PAY_AMT1` large). The model conservatively flags them due to payment delay, but the client managed to settle their balance.
  • False Negatives (Missed Defaulters): Clients with pristine zero-delay repayment histories (`PAY_1 = 0` or `-1`) who maintained low utilization until a sudden economic shock in September, defaulting with no prior delinquency signals.

Honest Project Limitations

  • No Date Column (Temporal Split Missing): The dataset does not provide calendar timestamps for transactions, preventing true out-of-time walk-forward validation.
  • Fixed Cost Ratio Simplification: We evaluate fixed cost ratios $r \in [1, 10]$. In real banking, losses scale continuously with client exposure ($c_{FN} \approx \text{Balance} \times \text{LGD}$).
  • Credit Limit Endogeneity: A client's credit limit reflects past approval policies of Taiwanese banks, functioning partially as an endogenous proxy.
